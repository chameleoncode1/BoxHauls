A half-ton pickup covers most single-item hauls — the real decision is whether your typical load justifies the extra payload (and extra fuel cost) of something bigger.

## Half-ton pickups (F-150 class)

The most common truck class for hauling gigs — enough payload for the vast majority of single-item and small-load hauls ([[TIER_1]] through most of [[TIER_2]]), with better fuel economy than heavier-duty trucks.

## Mid-size pickups (Tacoma class)

Smaller bed and lower payload than a half-ton, but better fuel economy and easier to maneuver — a reasonable fit if you're mostly taking lighter, smaller items and want to minimize running costs.

## Heavy-duty pickups (2500-class and up)

The highest payload capacity, built for consistently heavy loads — appliances, safes, larger [[TIER_3]] hauls. The tradeoff is worse fuel economy on every trip, including the lighter ones, which adds up over a lot of driving.

## Trailers

For occasional oversized loads, towing a trailer behind a smaller truck can extend what you're able to carry without committing to a heavier-duty truck's fuel cost on every single haul.
