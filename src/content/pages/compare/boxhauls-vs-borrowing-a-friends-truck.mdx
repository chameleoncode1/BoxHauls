Borrowing a friend's truck costs nothing upfront, but it depends entirely on someone being available, willing, and comfortable lending you their vehicle — and there's no coverage if something goes wrong.

## The real cost

The truck itself might be free, but you're still paying in gas, your own time loading and driving, and the social cost of the favor — plus the risk of scheduling around someone else's availability instead of your own.

## What happens if something breaks

If an item or the truck itself gets damaged during a favor move, there's no insurance policy behind it — just whatever you and your friend work out afterward. A BoxHauls haul is covered by [[INSURANCE_CARRIER]] up to [[COVERAGE_LIMIT]] while your item is in the truck.

## Availability

A friend's truck is only an option if someone you know actually has one and is free at the time you need it — not always the case, especially on short notice.

## Where a friend's truck wins

For a quick, light, one-time favor with someone genuinely willing and available, free is hard to beat, and there's no substitute for trusting someone you know.

## Where BoxHauls wins

When you need it now, need coverage if something goes wrong, or simply don't want to spend a favor — a booked haul doesn't depend on anyone else's schedule.

## Verdict

If a friend is truly free and willing, it's a fine option for something small. For anything bigger, time-sensitive, or where you'd rather not risk a friendship over a scratched dresser, see [pricing](/pricing/).
