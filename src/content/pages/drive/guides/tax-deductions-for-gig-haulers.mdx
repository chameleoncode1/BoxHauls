Gig haulers typically have three broad deduction categories to track: vehicle costs, hauling equipment, and work-related phone use — details depend on your specific situation.

## Vehicle costs

The two general methods are mileage tracking or actual-expense tracking (fuel, maintenance, depreciation) for the business-use portion of your vehicle. Check the current IRS standard mileage rate directly at irs.gov, since it changes periodically — this page won't quote a number that could go stale.

## Hauling equipment

Gear bought specifically for hauling — straps, moving blankets, a dolly, a loading ramp — is generally a legitimate business expense. See [driver equipment](/drive/equipment/) for the typical gear list.

## Phone and data

If you use your phone specifically for hauling work — the app, navigation, messaging riders — a business-use portion of that cost is a common deduction category, though the exact treatment depends on your situation.

## Keep records as you go

Log expenses and save receipts as they happen rather than reconstructing them at tax time — a simple folder or spreadsheet is enough for most drivers.

## This isn't tax advice

This page describes general deduction categories relevant to 1099 hauling work — it isn't advice for your specific situation. Talk to a tax professional for anything specific to you.
