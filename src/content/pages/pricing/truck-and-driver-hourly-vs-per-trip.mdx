BoxHauls prices per trip, not by the hour. Here's how that compares to hourly truck-and-driver pricing for different job sizes.

## The BoxHauls formula

[[BASE_FARE]] base fare plus [[PER_MILE]] per mile, shown before you book — no clock running, no charge for time spent parking or waiting.

## One item

For a single item (a couch, an appliance), per-trip pricing is almost always cheaper than hourly. You're paying for the trip itself, not a driver's minimum hour block.

| Distance | Per-trip cost |
|---|---|
| 3 miles | $26.30 |
| 10 miles | $48.00 |
| 25 miles | $94.50 |

## Five items

A handful of items in one load still fits a single trip in most cases — same per-trip pricing applies, possibly with [[TIER_2]] or [[TIER_3]] if the items are bulky.

## A full studio

A studio apartment often needs one to two trips (see [cost to move a studio apartment](/pricing/cost-to-move-a-studio-apartment/)). At this size, per-trip pricing is still typically cheaper than an hourly crew, since the load/unload work is quick for one or two people.

## Where hourly can win

Once a job needs three or more trips' worth of loading and unloading — a full one-bedroom or larger, with lots of stops and stairs — the total time an hourly crew spends may cost less than stacking per-trip fares. That's the point to get a real hourly quote and compare it directly against your expected trip count. See [BoxHauls vs. hiring movers](/compare/boxhauls-vs-hiring-movers/) for the fuller comparison.

## Bottom line

For single items and small loads, per-trip pricing wins. For large, multi-stop moves, compare directly — it can go either way depending on your specific job.
