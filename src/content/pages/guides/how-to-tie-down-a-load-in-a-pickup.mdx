Tying down a load properly means ratchet straps, real anchor points, and a tension check after the first few miles.

[PHOTO: ratchet straps crossing over a secured load in a pickup bed]

## Load heaviest items first

Place the heaviest items low and toward the front of the bed, near the cab, for better weight distribution and control.

## Ratchet straps, not rope

Ratchet straps hold tension reliably. Rope stretches and loosens during the drive, which can let a load shift.

## Use real anchor points

Attach straps to the truck bed's built-in tie-down anchors, not improvised spots like mirrors, bumpers, or door handles.

## Cross-strap

Crossing straps at an angle resists both forward and side-to-side shifting better than straight-back straps alone.

## Recheck after a few miles

A load can settle and loosen straps slightly once you're moving — check and re-tighten after the first few miles.
