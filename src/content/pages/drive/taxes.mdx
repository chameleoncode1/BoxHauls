As an independent contractor, BoxHauls doesn't withhold anything from your payout — reporting and paying your own taxes is on you, and a tax professional is worth the cost to get it right.

## 1099 status

Drivers are independent contractors, not employees. BoxHauls doesn't withhold income tax, Social Security, or Medicare from your payout — you're responsible for reporting that income and paying what you owe.

## Mileage vs. actual expenses

There are two general methods for deducting vehicle costs: tracking mileage driven for hauls, or tracking your actual vehicle expenses (fuel, maintenance, depreciation) and deducting a business-use percentage. Check the current IRS standard mileage rate directly at irs.gov before using that method, since it changes periodically — this page won't quote a specific rate that could go stale.

## Deductible gear

Equipment bought specifically for hauling — straps, moving blankets, a dolly, a ramp — is generally a legitimate business expense. See [driver equipment](/drive/equipment/) for the typical gear list and rough costs.

## Quarterly estimated taxes

Independent contractors generally need to pay estimated taxes quarterly rather than waiting until year-end, to avoid a penalty. Whether that applies to your specific situation depends on your total income and other factors.

## Talk to a professional

This page describes the general shape of 1099 work — it isn't tax advice for your specific situation. A tax professional can tell you exactly what applies to you.
